# Dankrupt — Launch Marketing Playbook

Nov 24, 2026 · @Faaz

Dankrupt's best marketing asset is the game itself: every day the meme market produces crashes, comebacks and whale moves that are already content. Before launch, the story is you building it in public. After launch, the market writes the posts.

## 1. Strategy at a glance

**Audience:** meme-literate 16 to 28 year olds who live on Reels and Shorts, plus finance-curious people who already joke about "stonks". English first, with India and global audiences together.

**Positioning:** "The stock market, but the companies are memes." One line, no explanation needed.

**The two engines:**

| Engine | When | What it posts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build in public | Now until launch | You building the game: features, bugs, maths, decisions the audience votes on |
| The market is the content | After launch | Daily market recaps, crashes, comebacks, player wins and losses, leaderboards |

**What platforms reward right now:**

- **Instagram Reels:** ranking order is watch time and completion first, then sends per reach, then likes per reach ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/es/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)). DM shares matter most for reaching new people, so design posts people want to send to a friend.
- **YouTube Shorts:** each Short is tested on a small seed audience first, and if retention is weak the push stops ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/tr/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm)). The first 2 to 3 seconds decide almost everything.
- **Devlogs work for indie games:** creators recommend 3 to 5 short clips a week plus longer YouTube devlogs ([GTStu](https://gtstu.com/?p=5085)). You're planning 2 to 3 a day, which is more than enough if quality holds.

**The rule for every post:** would someone send this to a friend? If not, rework the hook or don't post it.

## 2. Content pillars and hook bank

Five pillars keep 2 to 3 posts a day varied without running out of ideas. Every post opens on its most surprising frame, not on you introducing yourself.

| Pillar | Share of posts | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build in public | 30% | Feature demos, bugs, maths explained simply, "day 14 of building" |
| Community decides | 20% | Polls and votes that really change the game (section 3) |
| Meme market news | 20% | Treat real memes like stocks: "this meme is crashing, here's the chart" |
| Characters and skits | 15% | Recurring original characters reacting to the market |
| Player stories | 15% | After launch: biggest win, worst loss, wildest comeback of the day |

### Hook bank

Copy these for the first 1 to 2 seconds. Text on screen plus a strong visual, never a slow intro.

Build in public:

1. "I'm building a stock market where the companies are memes."
2. "This bug just made one meme worth 4 million coins."
3. "I wrote the maths that decides when a meme dies."
4. "My game just bankrupted its first player. It was me."
5. "Day 1 vs day 30 of building a meme stock exchange."

Meme market news (works before launch, using real attention data):

6. "This meme just went up 400% and nobody noticed."
7. "If this meme were a stock, you'd be broke right now."
8. "Rating this week's memes as investments."
9. "The meme graveyard: memes that will never come back. Or will they?"
10. "Buy, hold or sell: \[meme name\]."

Player and market stories (after launch):

11. "Someone turned 100,000 coins into 2 million in one night."
12. "This player shorted the wrong meme and lost everything."
13. "A dead meme just came back from the grave. Holders are rich."
14. "The whale who moved the whole market."
15. "Today's market in 30 seconds."

Share triggers (for DM sends):

16. "Send this to the friend who'd go bankrupt first."
17. "Tag the person who called this meme early."
18. "Which meme is your friend? Send it to them."

## 3. Interactive story series

Your vote-what-happens-next idea fits Dankrupt perfectly, because a market is already a story where every decision has a visible outcome. The flagship is a fictional trader whose every move the audience votes on, and who becomes a real account in the game at launch.

Two rules from people who run these formats:

- **Make the result visible.** Audiences stop voting once they learn their vote changes nothing ([JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/poll-game-on-instagram/)). Always post the outcome the next day.
- **Keep each Story run short.** Viewing drops off after about four to six Story frames ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/instagram-story-formats)). Aim for 4 to 6 frames: recap, situation, vote, teaser.

### Series A: "Benny Bagholder" (flagship)

An original cartoon trader, drawn in the same sticker style as the logo, starts with 100,000 coins. Every day:

1. **Recap frame:** yesterday's vote and what happened to his portfolio, using real attention data on real memes.
2. **Situation frame:** "Benny is holding \[meme\]. It's up 40% but the chart looks tired."
3. **Vote frame:** poll sticker, for example "SELL / HOLD" or "SHORT IT / STAY OUT".
4. **Teaser frame:** "Results tomorrow. If he loses, he goes Dankrupt."

Why it works: it teaches the game without a tutorial, it builds a character people root for, and a bankruptcy is a guaranteed viral episode. At launch, Benny becomes a real in-game account run by community votes, so followers can watch "their" portfolio on the leaderboard.

Once a week, cut the best episode into a Reel ending on the cliffhanger: "Vote in my Stories." That pulls Reels viewers into Stories.

### Series B: "Meme Court" (weekly)

Followers nominate memes in a question sticker, and a Reel shows the top four as "defendants". Stories then run a bracket of polls, and the winner is promised a real listing at launch. This builds the launch meme list with the audience, so they arrive already invested in it.

### Series C: "Moon or Graveyard" (daily, 1 frame)

One real meme, one poll: will its attention go up or down by tomorrow? The next day, show the real attention chart and the community's running hit rate, for example "Community: 7 right, 3 wrong." It's a cheap daily habit, and it proves trend-spotting is a real skill.

### Series D: "Build it or bin it" (2 to 3 a week)

Vote on real design decisions, such as logo variants, feature names, Graveyard art or the first badges. Every feature that ships gets a Reel: "You voted for this. Here it is."

### Series E: Cliffhanger Reels

A Reel follows a meme's chart rising and freezes at the peak: "Crash or comeback? Answer in the comments." Post the answer as a follow-up Reel two days later, linking to the first.

## 4. Platform playbook

Instagram and YouTube Shorts carry the reach, Discord holds the community, and Reddit and X bring the early adopters. TikTok is banned in India, so skip it unless you later have a teammate posting from abroad.

| Platform | Role | Daily output | What to post |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram Reels | Main reach engine | 1 to 2 Reels | Hooks from section 2; end with a share trigger |
| Instagram Stories | Community and votes | 4 to 6 frames | Benny, Moon or Graveyard, Build it or bin it |
| YouTube Shorts | Second reach engine | Same Reels, reposted without watermarks | Same cuts; test 15 to 35 seconds |
| YouTube long-form | Depth and trust | 1 a week, 8 to 15 min | Devlog: "I built the maths behind a meme stock market" |
| X | Builders and finance-meme crowd | 2 to 3 posts | Charts, GIFs, threads on the pricing maths |
| Reddit | Early adopters | 1 to 2 a week | Screenshot Saturday on r/gamedev, r/IndieGaming, r/webgames |
| Discord | Home base | Daily | Votes, beta access, bug reports, leaderboards |
| LinkedIn | Your personal brand | 1 to 2 a week | The engineering story: matching engine, maths, lessons |

### Instagram

- **Trial Reels:** they show a Reel to non-followers first and measure watch time and completion before deciding whether to push it to your followers ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/de/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)). They unlock at 1,000 followers. Use them to A/B test hooks: same video, two different first lines.
- **Original audio and no watermarks:** never repost TikTok-watermarked clips.
- **Close every Reel with a reason to DM it:** "send this to your friend who'd go bankrupt first."

### YouTube

- **Shorts:** the first 1 to 3 seconds decide distribution. Start around 15 to 35 seconds and let retention data guide you ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/get-more-views-youtube-shorts/)).
- **Long-form devlogs:** these build deeper fans. Someone who watches five devlog episodes is already invested ([GTStu](https://gtstu.com/?p=5085)). Cut each devlog into 3 to 5 Shorts.

### Reddit

One indie team found Reddit converted best, even though Instagram sent more visits ([WN Hub on Deep Pixel Melancholy](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-48313)). Post as a developer sharing work, follow each subreddit's self-promotion rules, and reply to every comment.

### Creators

Short-form creators usually drive the biggest spikes for indie games ([Javier Moreno Gaona](https://bg.linkedin.com/in/javier-moreno-gaona)). Before launch, list 50 to 100 meme, finance-meme and Indian gaming creators. Two weeks before launch, offer them early access, a custom meme listed in their name, or a creator league.

## 5. Production system

Two to three posts a day is sustainable only if posting runs off what you're already doing. Record while you build, batch the editing once a week, and post the same cut to every platform. After launch, let the game generate its own daily videos.

### The pipeline

1. **Capture while building:** keep a screen recorder running during dev sessions, and say one line on camera whenever something works or breaks. That's raw material, not extra work.
2. **One edit day a week:** cut 10 to 15 shorts from the week's footage in CapCut using fixed templates: same font, same colours as the logo, same captions style.
3. **One cut, every platform:** each short goes to Reels, Shorts and X, with no watermarks.
4. **Schedule it:** Meta Business Suite for Instagram and YouTube Studio for Shorts, both free.
5. **Stories stay live:** Benny and the polls are posted by hand each day, about 15 minutes.

### Weekly rhythm

| Day | Reels and Shorts | Stories | Other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Build in public + meme news | Benny + Moon or Graveyard | X thread |
| Tue | Hook test (two versions) | Benny + Build it or bin it | Discord vote |
| Wed | Meme news + skit | Benny + Moon or Graveyard | LinkedIn post |
| Thu | Build in public + cliffhanger | Benny + Meme Court nominations | X clip |
| Fri | Benny weekly episode Reel | Benny + Meme Court bracket | Reddit post |
| Sat | Meme news | Meme Court final | YouTube devlog |
| Sun | Weekly recap | Results of everything | Edit day |

### Automate it after launch

Build a small "Market Close" generator into the backend that turns engine data into a 20-second vertical video every evening: top gainer, biggest crash, whale of the day, Benny's portfolio. That's one free post a day forever, and the "I called it" share cards from the PRD add player-made posts on top.

### Time budget

About 1 to 1.5 hours a day plus one edit afternoon a week. If quality drops, cut volume first and keep the hooks sharp: one great Reel beats three weak ones.

## 6. Phases

Five phases, each with a goal that unlocks the next. Start phase 0 this week, because the audience has to exist before launch day.

1. **Phase 0, set up (this week)**
   - Claim @playdankrupt everywhere, and put up a one-page site with a waitlist and Discord invite.
   - Start Moon or Graveyard and the first build-in-public Reels.
   - Gate: handles claimed, waitlist live.
2. **Phase 1, build in public (until the closed alpha)**
   - 2 to 3 posts a day, Benny Bagholder running daily on real attention data.
   - Meme Court picks the launch memes.
   - Gate: 1,000 Instagram followers (unlocks Trial Reels) and 500 on the waitlist.
3. **Phase 2, closed alpha**
   - 50 to 100 testers from Discord, each with a "Founding Trader" badge.
   - Their wins, losses and bug finds become content.
   - Gate: the engine survives real players, and you have 20 or more player stories.
4. **Phase 3, launch week**
   - 7-day countdown in Stories, creator posts landing on day 1.
   - Opening bell livestream: the Meme Court winners IPO live, and Benny's account trades its first community vote.
   - Referrals give badges, never coins, so leaderboards stay fair.
5. **Phase 4, after launch**
   - Daily auto-generated Market Close videos, weekly friend leagues, creator leagues.
   - Monthly events around big meme moments.
   - Gate for scaling: day-7 retention, not follower count.

## 7. Rules, metrics and sources

### Rules that keep the account safe

- **Never imply real money.** No "earn money", "get rich" or rupee and dollar signs; say "coins" and "paper money", and put "Free. No real money, ever." in every bio. India's gaming law covers advertising of money games too, so the marketing must look like a game, not a casino.
- **Memes are other people's work.** Show them as commentary, credit creators where known, and take down anything on request. Never mock private individuals.
- **No engagement bait.** Asking for likes or follows in manipulative ways can be penalised on Instagram ([Later](https://later.com/blog/instagram-algorithm/amp)). Ask for sends and votes, which are real actions.
- **Label paid creator posts** as ads, following ASCI's influencer disclosure rules in India.

### Metrics that matter

| Metric | Target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3-second hold on Shorts and Reels | 70% or more | The first seconds decide distribution |
| Sends per reach (Instagram) | Rising week on week | Strongest signal for reaching new people |
| Story poll votes per view | 15% or more | Shows the community is really playing along |
| Followers to waitlist | 5% or more | Turns attention into players |
| Day-7 retention after launch | 25% or more | The only number that says the game works |

Review every Sunday: keep the formats that hit targets, drop the bottom third, and test two new hooks.
